By seven in the morning, the first light is on the mountains and Calle Roma
smells like warm bread and hosed-down cobblestones. The gas truck sings its
jingle a street over, shopkeepers and neighbors sweep their stretches of
sidewalk, and the comales — the flat griddles of the local kitchens — are
ticking as they heat. This is Versalles: one of Vallarta's oldest
neighborhoods, named after the palace in France with streets named after
European cities, ten minutes from the airport and nothing like the resort
strip.

Nobody in Versalles performs for visitors — everyone here is simply part of
the place. The neighborhood became, steadily and quietly, where Vallarta
itself goes out to eat, and you can walk to more good kitchens, cafés and
fondas in five minutes than most towns get in total. In the evening the heat
lifts and the golden hour is made for walking: families take the sidewalks,
the parrots argue in the wires overhead, and dinner is a decision you make
by wandering until your nose votes. By the second week, the owner at
Ascencio's and the clerk at Oh! Croissant will know your order.

Apartment 303 is the corner unit on the third floor of Roma 240, and its
balcony wraps the cobblestone corner of Calle Roma and Calle España.
Mornings out there are shaded and cool — coffee holds until nearly noon —
and from early afternoon the sun swings around and stays until it sets,
filtered through the crown of the big tree at the corner and the palms
below. The apartment is furnished for staying, not passing through: a
kitchen you can actually cook in, a dining table where you can watch the
sunset, a bed you would keep at home. Most of our guests are here for a
month or a season — snowbirds sitting out the Canadian and American winter,
families up from Guadalajara, professionals working from abroad who want to
live well on a budget. It is not the place for weekend partiers or
bargain-hunting overnighters — and we like it that way.

One thing to know before your first dinner: tostadas de marlín ahumado —
smoked marlin, the Bahía de Banderas staple that rarely survives contact
with a resort menu. Order them at Ocho Tostadas, standing at the barra like
everyone around you. And when you want one proper dinner out, NUMŌH is,
hands down, our favorite restaurant in the city.

The beach is a flat fifteen-minute walk west. The neighborhood starts at
your door.
