### A typical morning in Colonia Versalles

By seven in the morning — half an hour after the first light clears the
Sierra Madre — Calle Roma begins to stir. The street wakes the way it has
for decades: red tile roofs and whitewashed walls catching the early sun,
cobblestones hosed down and drying, and the smell of warm bread drifting
from the corner. A gas truck sings its jingle a street over, shopkeepers
sweep their stretches of sidewalk, and the *comales* — the flat griddles of
the local kitchens — are ticking as they heat.

### This is Versalles

This is one of Puerto Vallarta's oldest neighborhoods, named after the
palace in France, its streets after European cities, shaded by old parota
and mango trees. The families around you have lived on these blocks for
generations. And it is just ten minutes from the airport and nothing like
the resort strip — if your idea of a ruined afternoon is a crowded
boardwalk and a persistent timeshare agent, you are in the right place.

Over the years, quietly, Versalles became the neighborhood where Vallarta
itself goes out to eat. You can walk to more good kitchens, cafés and
*fondas* in five minutes than most towns have in total. In the evening the
heat lifts and the golden hour is ideal for walking: neighbors pull chairs
onto the sidewalk, families stroll by, parrots argue in the wires overhead,
and dinner is a decision you make by wandering until your nose votes. By
your second week, the owner at Ascencio Tacos y Chelas and the barista at
Oh! Croissant will know your name and your favorite order.

### Enjoying your stay at Apartment 303

When you are ready to wind down, home is close by: Apartment 303, a roomy
corner unit on the third floor of Roma 240, with its balcony wrapping the
cobblestone corner of Calle Roma and Calle España. Mornings out there are
shaded and cool — coffee holds until nearly noon — and from early afternoon
the sun swings around and stays until it sets, filtered through the canopy
of the old parota on the corner and the palm trees below. Inside, the
apartment is furnished for staying, not just passing through: a kitchen you
can actually cook in, a dining table where you can watch the sunset, and a
bed you would keep at home.

Most of our guests stay for a month or a season — snowbirds sitting out the
Canadian and American winter, families visiting from Guadalajara, or
professionals working from abroad who want to live well at a reasonable
price. It is not the place for weekend partiers or bargain-hunting
overnighters — and we like it that way.

### Our favorites — local tips

One thing to know before your first beach day: *tostadas de marlín ahumado*
— smoked marlin, a Bahía de Banderas staple rarely found on a resort menu.
Stop by Ocho Tostadas at noon and eat them sitting at the *barra*, like
everyone around you. And when you want one proper dinner out, NUMŌH is,
hands down, our favorite restaurant in Versalles and beyond. The beach is a
flat fifteen-minute walk west. The neighborhood starts right at your door.
